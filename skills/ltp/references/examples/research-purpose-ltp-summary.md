# Research group purpose: LTP summary

On 22–23 October 2026 the research stewards worked through one question: **what is the research group for, and are we moving towards it?** The stewards are Rufus Pollock, Bob Bunge (lead steward) and Jonah Wilberg. They used a Logical Thinking Process (LTP), a structured method that goes goal → what's wrong today → the conflicts underneath → the changes to make → a plan. They signed off the result on 23 October 2026 as their working position. It is a working bet, to be reviewed at the quarterly reflection.

## TL;DR

- **Goal:** clarify the paradigm shift, and seed that clarity into the Seeds of Renaissance (SoR) community and the wider culture.
- **Core problem:** good conversations rarely become finished, published work that travels. The group posts little medium-form writing, nobody actively distributes what it does publish, the forum can't surface quality, and attention is split across channels. The first two have a deeper cause: too few contributors, because there are no simple procedures volunteers can pick up.
- **What we'll do:**
  - one public "front of house" (Substack) plus an informal back room;
  - an outputs crew, partly paid, that turns calls into posts;
  - a ladder from open participation up to publishing;
  - a living map of our research questions.
- **When:** as soon as possible, from October 2026. Tracked in Beads epic `sorr-l57` in this repo.

## Current reality

**Undesirable effects:** what we see today, measured against the goal.

1. Few research outputs get finished; most work stays as talks.
2. Finished work isn't reliably published or findable.
3. The Friday speaker series leaves no lasting written record.
4. No agreed set of research questions, so sessions don't build on each other.
5. Research ideas barely reach SoR, and no audience beyond it has been chosen.
6. Steward capacity is thin: Bob is lead steward, and Jonah has less capacity at the moment.
7. Most of the 8–15 regulars rarely move from attending to contributing.
8. No budget for finishing or editing work.
9. Members weigh outputs and conversation differently, which splits effort.
10. The group doesn't grow beyond the regulars as much as it could.
11. The forum doesn't accumulate discussion or draw new people.
12. Too few high-quality participants; growth is slow.
13. We don't track or learn from how things are going.

**Causes** (how the effects connect):

```
C4 no simple SOPs for volunteers
 └─► C3 few contributors ──┬─► 1 few outputs finished
                           ├─► 7 attend, don't contribute
                           ├─► RC1 little medium-form posting
                           └─► RC2 no active distribution
RC4 no channel focus ──────┬─► RC1
                           └─► RC2
RC1 ─► effects 2, 3, 5        RC2 ─► effects 5, 10, 11
C2 awkward forum platform ─► RC3 no voting ─► 7, 11, 12
C1 operations friction ─► 6 thin steward capacity
```

Effects 4, 8, 9 and 13 have no traced cause. The changes below address them directly.

**The flywheel we want:** good posts travel, newcomers arrive and comment, good commenters rise into view, and some start posting their own work, which means more good posts. Today the wheel doesn't turn.

## Conflict clouds

Five tensions came up. Each looked like a choice between two good things, and each was resolved by finding an assumption that didn't hold. The change that resolves a cloud is called an *injection*.

**1. Where does our work go?**

```
     ┌─ B: Focus limited public ── D: One primary public
A:   │    energy in one place        channel
The ─┤                                    ▲ conflict
fly- │                                    ▼
wheel└─ C: An informal back ────── D′: A separate informal
spins     room to jam in               space (the forum)
```

- **Broken assumption:** "two places split focus". That's only true if both places do the same job.
- **Injection:** front of house and back of house. There is one moderated public channel, where a small editor group posts, and the forum becomes the informal back room. Since 26 October the front of house has been Substack: this was agreed in [LTP 2](../projects/2026-10-research-community/) and will be confirmed once decision rights are settled.

**2. Conversation or outputs?**

```
     ┌─ B: Ideas persist and ──── D: Spend scarce hours on
A:   │    travel                    finished outputs
Seed ┤                                    ▲ conflict
the  │                                    ▼
shift└─ C: Real collective ────── D′: Spend scarce hours on
          cognition happens            live dialogue
```

- **Broken assumptions:** "it's the same few people's hours", and "outputs need separate work on top of dialogue". Rufus judged the second the key one.
- **Injection:** an outputs crew running simple SOPs (standard procedures). The crew harvests calls into written posts, supports original writing, and clips and reposts good material.

**3. Pay or volunteers for the crew?**
- **Broken assumption:** "no money is available". About $5–10k is likely raisable.
- **Injection:** a hybrid crew. A small paid coordinator runs volunteers on low-bono (reduced-fee) terms, with recognition such as bylines and the Fellow title. Plus: raise that funding.

**4. Open or selective membership?**
- **Broken assumption:** "membership is a single gate at entry". The noise comes from the forum having no voting, not from openness.
- **Injection:** a ladder. Anyone can comment. People who are upvoted become visible contributors, then Fellows, and Fellows earn the right to publish on the front of house.

**5. Free inquiry or an agreed agenda?**
- **Broken assumption:** "an agenda means directing people".
- **Injection:** a living question map. People work on anything, but on-map work is favoured for funded time. Each call's harvest is checked against the map, and the map is kept up to date with AI help.

## Future reality

| Injection | Effects it removes |
|---|---|
| Front of house / back of house | 2, 9; root cause RC4 |
| Outputs crew + SOPs | 1, 3, 5, 7; causes C3, C4 |
| Hybrid crew + funding | 8; supports 1 and 3 |
| Ladder | 7, 10, 11, 12 |
| Living question map | 4 |
| Recruit steward capacity | 6 (partly) |
| Throughput measures (a prerequisite, K1) | 13 |

All of these injections were agreed. Whether they remove the effects is a prediction to test. The predicted chain: harvest → posts finished → published → reposted → newcomers climb the ladder → Fellows join the crew.

**Risks checked:**
- More coordination load: answered by recruiting steward capacity.
- The map going stale: answered by maintaining it with AI.
- Funding running out: accepted as a risk, since the group runs lean.
- Ranking feeling exclusionary: accepted as a risk.

## Open questions

- **Which first audience beyond SoR?** Policy people, the public or scholars? This shapes formats and channels (NC1).
- **What editorial bar applies on the front of house?** Which outputs count, and what quality.
- **Are CSF3 and CSF4 truly necessary?** Their necessity hasn't been tested yet.
- **Does the flywheel actually spin?** The throughput measures will tell us.
