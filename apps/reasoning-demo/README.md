# Reason Commons in use: a site of commons

A static, read-only site at [`/demos/reason-commons-in-use/index.html`](../../website/demos/reason-commons-in-use/index.html) showing what a platform of commons could look like. A commons is a goal and the reasoning a group builds around it with the Reason Commons skill. The site lists Rufus and David's collaboration, the only commons with accepted reasoning; Gandhi 2.0, a proposed source reconstruction of a Second Renaissance Forum discussion; Re-membering, a proposed reconstruction of Margeret Heath’s supplied talks with PDF page references; and a clearly labelled fictional example, Willow Lane Primary. Every commons but Rufus and David's is entirely proposed, and the build refuses acceptance or dispute anywhere else. Nothing on the site edits a commons; contributions start from a copyable brief taken to the reader's own AI.

## Pages

| Page | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| `index.html` | What is a commons, and which ones can I read? |
| `how-it-works.html` | How is a commons built, what are the six questions, and what do the standings mean? This is the one page that names the Logical Thinking Process. |
| `<commons>/index.html` | The overview: the goal, what stands in the way, the conflict and the change chosen, actions, and who contributed and accepted. |
| `<commons>/map.html` | The six questions, each drawn small in its real shape. |
| `<commons>/goal.html`, `in-the-way.html`, `conflict.html`, `change.html` | One question each: a diagram read in that tree's direction, an outline view, and the statements no connection reaches yet. |
| `<commons>/prerequisites.html`, `actions.html` | What has to happen first, and who does what: the steps in stages from left to right, and the actions in order. Each card says who proposed it and who has accepted it; selecting one opens a read-only panel. |
| `<commons>/items.html` | Every statement and connection. `items.html#<id>` shows one item on its own (CSS `:target`), with a diagram of its neighbours, its contributors and endorsers, assumptions, challenges, open work, history and a brief for the skill. |
| `<commons>/questions.html`, `learning.html` | Open questions, and expected-versus-reported outcomes. Generated only when the commons publishes them, which today means only the example. |
| `<commons>/changes.html`, `about.html` | Releases with earlier wording kept, and what the record is and isn't. |

Diagrams are static SVG laid out at build time in `diagrams.py`: layered for the trees, the classic five-box arrangement for a conflict, and role groups for statements without connections. A strict hierarchy of "is needed for" links (one parent each, no joint conditions or loops, which today means every goal tree) reads top to bottom instead: the root first, each child indented beneath its parent and joined to it by a rail, at most 636px wide however many conditions there are. Such a diagram can be stepped through: a Step through switch shows only the goal, and selecting a box (click, Enter or Space) opens the level beneath it, while selecting it again folds its branch. A counter, Show all and Start over sit beside the switch, the page scrolls only as far as needed to keep the opened box and its new children in view, and new rows fade in unless reduced motion is set. Stepping starts on for trees of more than 10 statements and each reader's choice is remembered in their browser; without JavaScript the whole tree shows. The other trees keep the layered layout, because indenting shared causes or joint conditions would duplicate boxes or split a joint condition. Every arrow comes from a published link; a link with several sources meets at one "and" junction before its single arrow. Text is wrapped with measured Alegreya Sans advance widths, so it cannot overflow its box. The font files are the unchanged Latin subsets from the reference pages, under the SIL Open Font License (`fonts/OFL.txt`).

Plans read differently. On What has to happen first? the steps run from left to right in stages: a step's stage is the longest chain of "is needed for" links before it, so stage 1 holds what nothing else is needed for (Now), and each later stage what becomes possible once the one before it is done (Next, Then, Later). A stage is not a date. Every step overcomes its obstacle, so the obstacle sits on the step's card as "Gets past" instead of in a box of its own. The critical path is meant as in project planning, the steps with no slack before the furthest objective, and is drawn only when it singles something out; when every chain is equally long, nothing is marked. On Who does what? each action is a numbered row read from its starting point to its expected effect, with who ("Who: unassigned" and any proposed role) and the steps it delivers. Actions are grouped into the same Now, Next and Later stages when the record orders them, through "comes before" links or one action starting from another's effect; a feedback link closes a loop and orders nothing. When no order is recorded, as in Gandhi 2.0, a timeline places each action by the stages of the steps it delivers. Both pages use one grammar for standing: dashed outline proposed, solid accepted for now, dotted clay disputed, for cards, connections and the rule beside an obstacle alike, with hollow marks for whoever proposed an item and filled marks after a tick for whoever accepted it, square for the AI and round for a person. The cards are HTML laid out at build time by `stages`, `critical_path`, `arrange` and `action_steps` in `diagrams.py`; without JavaScript each card lists what it needs and links to its record, and every connection on the page carries its link ID. `app.js` draws the lines between stages, traces what a selected step needs (green) and makes possible (dark), opens the card's read-only panel (standing, people, the obstacle, each connection's own standing, the actions that deliver it, links into the other questions and sources), and adds the "Show the steps toward" filter, which re-orders each stage for one objective, and stage jumps. On a phone the stages stack from top to bottom.

## Build and check

From the public repository root:

```sh
python3 apps/reasoning-demo/build.py
python3 -m unittest discover -s apps/reasoning-demo -p 'test_*.py'
python3 apps/reasoning-demo/build.py --check
node --check apps/reasoning-demo/app.js
python3 scripts/export-website.py
python3 scripts/check-website.py
python3 -m http.server 8878 --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory website
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8878/demos/reason-commons-in-use/index.html`. Every page is complete without JavaScript; `app.js` adds the diagram/outline switch, statement search, path highlighting, plan lines, tracing and detail panels, and clipboard copying, and redirects the earlier `?view=…&tree=…&item=…` links to the new pages. `--check` compares all 75 generated files and fails on unexpected files in the published folder. With PyYAML installed it also re-projects the fictional example and source reconstructions from their public working files and fails if any pinned snapshot has drifted.

## Records with distinct publication boundaries

**Ours.** The authoritative collaboration record is in the private product repository and is never copied here. `publication.json` holds the reviewed allowlist from the v2 record (`collab/v2/reason-commons.yaml`), approved display names and the pinned projection (projection version 1). The accepted part, 63 statements and 57 connections selected under `demo-v2-collab-source` revision 2, leaves out accepted reasoning about funding, money and Rufus and David's own working arrangements; every accepted item is accepted by Rufus, and David has not yet recorded acceptance in v2. Under `proposed-reasoning-public` revision 1 (release 2026-10-26.2) the allowlist adds every proposed item: 62 statements and 39 connections across current reality, conflict, future reality, prerequisites and actions, funding and working arrangements included, published without endorsers and drawn as proposed. A connection is published only when all its ends are, which leaves out two proposed connections that reach excluded accepted items. The prerequisites and actions pages are entirely proposed. The first release, from the earlier record, was accepted as `accepted-reasoning-demo-2026-10-22` revision 1 and the redesign as `commons-site-redesign-2026-10-22` revision 1. Routine builds and CI use the pinned projection, so they need no private access. For an authorized refresh:

```sh
python3 apps/reasoning-demo/build.py --refresh /absolute/path/to/authorized/reason-commons.yaml
```

The refresh validates the working state with the skill's validator, then exports only the selected fields of the selected items. An accepted item keeps its named endorsers and recorded context; a proposed item is published with none, and the refresh fails if a selected item is disputed, lists endorsers while proposed, or is an accepted connection whose ends are no longer all accepted. Unselected reasoning, assumptions, challenges, backlog, outcome reports, receipts, paths and notes stay private. Publishing any of them needs its own selection approval.

**The fictional example.** `examples/willow-lane/reason-commons.yaml` is an invented working file in the skill's conversational format, checked by the skill's own validator, with feedback snapshots that match its statements exactly. `examples/willow-lane/publication.json` selects its items and adds display names, roles and an authored release history. Its projection (version 2) keeps assumptions, challenges, open work, cross-tree traces and reports, and is accepted by the build only when `is_example` is true. Since `proposed-reasoning-public` revision 1 it is entirely proposed: no statement or connection is accepted or disputed, its two challenges stay open on proposed items, its trial's snapshots record proposed reasoning (a report is not acceptance), and its release history records no acceptance or dispute. Every example page carries a "Fictional example" banner, its URL and data say `example-`, and its briefs say there is no shared record behind it. To change it, edit the working file and run:

```sh
python3 apps/reasoning-demo/build.py --refresh-example
```

**Gandhi 2.0.** `ltp/gandhi-2-0/reason-commons.yaml` is an AI reconstruction of the Second Renaissance Forum thread *What Would Make a Second Renaissance Possible?*, drawing on two supplied analyses of it. It has 233 proposed statements and 238 proposed connections: the analysis's goal map, causal tree, all five conflicts, ten changes with their effects, fifteen risks each with its remedy, the alternatives raised in the thread, thirty-six first steps and eight actions. Every item credits the forum posts or essays it draws on: a forum credit names a post and that post's author, an essay credit cites the section the analysis points to, and the analysis's own reconstructions and inferences say so. The AI stays the only contributor, because it selected, worded and connected everything; credits never become authorship or endorsement, and nothing is quoted. The registry at `commons/gandhi-2-0/publication.json` pins the selection, the thread's 36 posts with their authors and dates, reviewed source descriptions, titled views for every question but the goal, the overview's blocks, retired items and the release history. Each view draws its own statements plus every connection into them; a source that belongs to another part joins as a muted box, so a joint condition is never split. A statement may sit in more than one view, as in the analysis's own sub-trees. Projection version 4 permits only proposed AI reconstruction with no endorsers or feedback commitments/reports, and refuses a credit naming anyone other than the cited post's author, an essay not shared by its author, a statement left out of every view, an overview path step that is not a published connection, chain or cross-question reference, a published item recorded as retired, or a proposed role on anything but an action. It reuses the qualification checks without changing version 1's accepted-only boundary or version 2's fictional-example requirement. Every page has a source-reconstruction banner; inspect an item for its credits, linked posts and inference labels. Original documents, local paths, approval receipts and unselected fields are not exported. Refresh after an authorised edit:

```sh
python3 apps/reasoning-demo/build.py --refresh-reconstruction
```

The opening view is built from the registry's overview blocks: the six conditions, one path from a problem through its conflict and a change to the goal and a first action, the conflict behind central intervention with the other four in brief, a proposed comparison with its possible harm, an unassigned first task and a table of whose ideas the map draws on. Two relations draw differently: `feeds_back_to` returns round the right-hand side as a labelled loop and stays out of the layer ranking, and `prevents` ends in a bar at the risk a remedy is meant to prevent. One banner says that everything is proposed, so cards and diagram boxes carry credits instead of a standing badge, except on the two plan pages, where each card also says it is proposed by the AI and not accepted. Actions show "Who: unassigned" with the analysis's proposed role. There is no trial result, assigned host or participant endorsement. See [the source and review record](../../docs/product/gandhi-2-0-source-review.md).

**Re-membering.** `ltp/re-membering/reason-commons.yaml` reconstructs the practical argument of Margeret Heath’s *Being-Becoming With the World, Talk 1: Re-membering* from five directly supplied original PDFs: a 20-page slide deck, 33-page spoken script with notes, nine-page extended-passages companion, seven-page glossary of 72 terms and 14-page indexed bibliography. Margeret Heath is their sole credited author. Its 82 statements and 66 connections all remain proposed, attributed to the AI’s reconstruction, with no human endorsers or feedback commitments. Every item has a source locator and an explicit basis: paraphrase, AI reconstruction or AI inference. The source documents are companions, not independent corroboration; the script supplies the main evidence and consent qualifications, the glossary clarifies concepts, and the bibliography separates cited work from Heath’s syntheses. Its source-checking annotations are not independent verification by this commons.

The registry at `commons/re-membering/publication.json` selects the public items, five sources, sole author, titled views, overview blocks and three-release history. Its reviewed `presentation` fields override the site’s default summary, description and scope; identity and publication boundaries cannot be overridden. A `supplied_document` source has exactly `id`, `kind`, `title`, `description` and a known `author`; it requires no invented forum post, admits only its own reference bases and is credited as a directly read document. Existing forum-shared essays retain their original author/post checks and rendering. The original PDFs, local paths, receipts and private records never enter the projection. The shared publisher preserves the earlier accepted-only and fictional-example boundaries and the existing Gandhi 2.0 pages.

The opening now presents three attributed observations of Heath’s process account before one possible practical application. A reviewed `account` block is allowed only first, only for supplied documents and only for observation items; it uses existing cards and links to the songline, timing and enmindment explanation. The source observations have no invented causal links. Author-credit text acknowledges feedback on an earlier map while preserving unendorsed standing for the current revision. The learning view includes a reciprocal coordination–capacity loop, with new links explicitly sourced as AI inferences; some uncertainty may be generative, while novelty can also overwhelm or harm. Assessment can revise capacities and purposes as well as the next action.

All six views are populated: a goal and its necessary conditions, a connected candidate causal explanation, the stability–participation conflict, three routes to improvement with pressure and surveillance branches, candidate obstacles and readiness dependencies, and five illustrative actions. The practice and tool-review branches share readiness and proceed in parallel before assessment; a feedback connection returns assessment to mapping. Joint inputs stay whole across titled parts, and prevention remains a hypothesis. Conditions in the proposed action sequence are labelled “Illustrative condition” and actions “Who: unassigned”; neither observed conditions nor a real pilot is implied. Twenty-one review entries remain open; the questions page groups them by twenty referenced items. Source notes retain the distinctions between evidence for coupling, contested constitution of mind and the author’s participatory philosophical position. The six views are temporary analytical cuts through reciprocal processes across timescales. Re-project public reconstructions with the existing `--refresh-reconstruction` command after an authorised edit; routine CI reads only the pinned snapshots and public working files. See [the source and review record](../../docs/product/re-membering-source-review.md).

## Purpose, participation, sources and historical revisions

Each commons overview opens with a plain-language expression of its published goal, an explicit Get involved route (practice for the fictional example), and one question drawn from a public statement or connection. The reviewed `front_page` metadata in `site.json` is editorial presentation: it cannot alter reasoning, standing or endorsement, and its reference must be in the public projection. Selecting an invitation does not record a group-agreed priority. The exact goal and its standing remain inspectable, and all fuller overview material remains in a native, initially closed disclosure.

Get involved offers three concrete starts: an example, an assumption to question or help with interpretation. Each has a contextual brief for an AI and a script-free reading/copying fallback. Real commons also link to a prefilled draft in the public project issue tracker; a visitor reviews and posts it there. Nothing is sent automatically or incorporated into the reasoning by opening a link or copying a brief. The invented Willow Lane example provides practice only and has no sharing recipient. The contribution destination is explicitly allowlisted to the public project repository.

Source reconstructions retain compact provenance near the opening, full document representations in an initially closed source disclosure, and source cards alongside source descriptions on About. Cards lead to native source disclosures where availability, citation counts and linked examples retain every locator and attribution basis for that source. No original cover, document availability or independent corroboration is invented. The directory places the goal, featured question and involvement route before provenance.

Re-membering's `commons/re-membering/review.json` keeps its three already-public release snapshots, originating commit IDs and fingerprints. The authentic graph and wording comparisons and detailed releases remain separate, initially collapsed disclosures on Changes. Historical labels, wording and connections remain fixed to their own snapshots, with shared nodes in matching positions. The short overview feedback account leads to the comparison while keeping author judgment open; neither retraining nor endorsement is established. Public `reasoning.json` and the working reasoning are unchanged by the overview redesign.

Map pages retain their existing routes and all six question destinations, with the published goal as context. A file existing in Git is insufficient evidence of live delivery: after publication, inspect the actual Map and all six pages and reject JSON errors or incomplete HTML. The 25 October review found the live host missing these already-generated files; the hosting repair is tracked with the implementation and must be verified separately from local build success. Every generated HTML page carries CSS and JavaScript versions derived from their content. A changed asset therefore changes each page's delivery fingerprint while keeping its body and public reasoning intact. This both refreshes GitHub-driven publication and avoids stale asset references.

## Meaning the site preserves

- Standing is spelled out and shown by border style as well as colour: accepted for now (solid), proposed (dashed), disputed (dotted). Only Rufus and David's commons records acceptance. Earlier acceptance of a disputed item is shown as history, never as current acceptance. Where every item has the same standing, as in a source reconstruction, one banner states it instead of a badge on each item, except on the plan pages, where every card keeps the same marks for who proposed and who accepted it.
- Contribution and acceptance are separate. AI-originated ideas are attributed to the AI; imported statements with no known author say so. In a source reconstruction, a credit says whose post or essay an idea comes from; it is neither contribution to the commons nor agreement with the AI's wording.
- Our record draws accepted connections as accepted and proposed ones as proposed, and groups statements without a published connection by role, with no arrows. Qualified public records draw proposed connections as proposed, including joint conditions. No constraint, root cause, sequence or evidence rating is inferred from layout.
- Outcome verdicts keep their meaning: observed, not observed, unclear, not assessed and awaiting a report stay distinct, and no trial is called a success.
- The example's people, places and reports are invented. Nothing in it is evidence about a real project.

## Release and rollback

Publish only `website/`. Run the build, tests, drift and website checks, then use the existing separate Flowershow preview target when authenticated; do not change production settings to test this page. Rollback is a reviewed revert of the demo change on the publishing branch. ## Wisdom and AI

`commons/wisdom-ai/publication.json` registers the public [Wisdom and AI](https://ai.lifeitself.org/) research agenda and David Joseph’s supplied analysis of the first map. `ltp/wisdom-ai/reason-commons.yaml` now records 72 proposed statements and 42 proposed links across all six trees. The original release of seven statements and four links is preserved, including its wording, topology and AI attribution, in the registry’s fingerprinted `previous_releases`. The Changes page reads the actual archived wording and offers the original public snapshot, so history does not silently acquire today’s goal or attribution. No private working record is included.

The explicitly selected `commons/wisdom-ai/source-questions.json` companion remains unchanged: the governing question and 93 entries across alignment, risk and pace, broader impacts and consciousness. The research page supplies questions, not answers or empirical support. Its `web_page` references allow inference or a related question; they cannot be presented as source-author assertions. Exact question locators are checked. David’s separate `supplied_analysis` source refers to sections 1–6 of `docs/marketing/demos/wisdom-ai-analysis.md`, preserving the submitted text and its attribution. The AI is credited for its retained initial proposals and capture, rather than replacing the originator of ideas supplied by David.

Projection version 4 remains proposed-only, with no endorsers, feedback commitments or results. Its default AI-only contribution boundary is unchanged for other reconstructions. A registry may explicitly opt into `contribution_sources` for a named participant whose display name matches a reviewed supplied-analysis author. A human contribution then needs a matching source reference with basis `paraphrase` or `reconstruction`; an inference, unrelated question, unselected author or absent policy cannot supply that authority. Historical snapshots validate against their own authors, selection and contribution policy, without inheriting the current one. The accepted-only and fictional-example boundaries remain unchanged.

All six views use the existing diagram geometry, fonts, colours, standing treatments, item details, outline switch and copyable briefs. The causal branches are hypotheses; joint causes retain AND junctions. Review points need usable evidence, authority and incentives, and feasible alternatives; formal review also has a proposed negative branch through unwarranted reassurance to premature action. Preconditions and steps are conditional drafts. The capability, actor, wisdom-derived practice, setting, owner and date remain unspecified; supporting empirical evidence and action commitments are explicitly absent. A stronger diagnosis can lead to rejection of review points.

After a deliberate edit, refresh only this registry with `refresh_reconstruction` from `build.py`, preserving the fingerprinted earlier release. Run the normal build, full tests and `--check`. The generic `--refresh-reconstruction` option refreshes every reconstruction, so use it only when all those sources are intended to be refreshed. Keep the original capture date and SHA-256 unless the research page itself is recaptured. Update the authored release history for material changes. The earlier `/demos/wisdom-ai/index.html` route continues to redirect to the shared overview.
