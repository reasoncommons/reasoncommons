## What changed

The goal now addresses defensible aims, risks and responsibilities, with branches for alignment, risk and pace, broader impacts and consciousness, plus understanding, revision and restraint. The proposed necessary conditions require justification; they are not asserted to suffice for wisdom.

The current-reality map keeps uncertainty as one hypothesis alongside contested ends and implicit values; competitive pressure, perceived disadvantage and weak coordination; delegation and reduced opportunities to practise judgment; and moral uncertainty with absent criteria. Joint causes remain joint. Alternatives include uncertainty encouraging restraint and AI assistance strengthening judgment. No observations establish which problems occur in a particular setting or what sustains them.

The candidate conflict contrasts releasing a capability now with deferral, behind needs for timely benefits/security and protection against unacceptable harm. The actor, capability, release decision and time horizon remain unselected; relevant participants have not recognised the cloud. Its assumptions ask whether narrower capabilities, restricted access, safeguards or coordination could meet both needs, and whether slowing creates other serious risks.

The direct review-to-revision link now needs four joint conditions: review points, usable evidence, authority and incentives, and feasible alternatives. Further branches propose explicit ethical aims, a specified wisdom-derived practice and proportionate safeguards or coordination. A negative branch connects formal review, unwarranted reassurance and premature action. Two design requirements address those risks: substantive criteria cannot be replaced by completion of review, and safeguards must precede potentially irreversible action. The relationships remain hypotheses, and the practice, mechanism, effect and setting still require specification. Review points may be rejected if a stronger diagnosis or intervention emerges.

The prerequisite tree records a bounded objective and candidate obstacles, intermediate objectives and dependencies. The transition tree gives five conditional stages, each with current premise plus proposed action leading to an expected effect. Its “existing conditions” are conditional premises for the plan, not reports that testing has occurred or results exist. Owners and dates are unassigned. A test must be justified, authorised and safe; if a safe test is unavailable, gather information or improve safeguards instead. No trial or outcome-report commitment is invented.
