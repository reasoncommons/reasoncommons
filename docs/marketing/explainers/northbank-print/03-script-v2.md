# Keeping the promise at Northbank Print

Northbank Print has twenty-two people and a problem its customers can feel: orders go out after the promised date. Maya, the managing director, asks Stephen to help. Faster production sounds useful. Imani, an operator and staff representative, asks who will absorb the extra work.

Stephen asks what customers should notice, and what employees must be able to rely on. The team chooses a shared goal: dispatch standard repeat orders when promised, within paid working time, while people can challenge unrealistic commitments without penalty. The representatives check the wording with their teams. Bespoke work stays outside the twelve-week engagement.

The baseline shows twenty late orders out of eighty: one in four. Only twelve of forty changed-order requests receive an authorized answer, communicated to the customer, by the next working day. An acknowledgment alone does not count.

To finish, they will need two consecutive qualifying fortnights: no more than ten percent late and at least eighty percent prompt decisions. Overtime across all twenty-two employees must stay within eighteen hours in each week, with no unpaid work. At least sixteen of twenty eligible employees must say they can speak up, and at least eighteen must respond.

Stephen gathers interviews, records and an optional questionnaire. Ben's customer office faces urgent deadlines; Rosa's workshop faces materials and production limits. They consider coordination, adaptability, robustness and the changing environment before choosing where to start.

The first explanation says sales shares changes slowly, leaving instructions incomplete. Ben challenges the connection with timestamped emails. The group traces three changed orders before assigning a sales fix.

On two of those orders, the information arrived promptly. The wait came afterward: only Maya could approve the changed delivery commitment, and nobody covered her absence. Some jobs entered production before that decision was settled.

Stephen redraws the connection. A waiting decision, together with releasing the job before resolution, can leave instructions incomplete. Those instructions can contribute to late dispatches. Neither condition alone establishes this mechanism, and it does not explain every late order. Idle press slots also weaken a blanket machinery-shortage explanation.

The group chooses decision-making as its first coordination issue. Reviewing results will help it adapt; cover for Maya matters to robustness. Ten more order traces will test whether the issue is worth trying to change.

An ACT-informed discussion explores values, difficult thoughts and habitual responses. Ben values dependability but fears losing a customer when he asks for time. Rosa sometimes expects her objections to be dismissed. Imani points out that some requested dates really are impossible. Personal disclosure is voluntary; private sheets stay private.

They choose a morning review of changed orders. Maya expects to attend and authorize answers. The hoped-for sequence is earlier discussion, prompt decisions, complete instructions and fewer late dispatches. Preparing for the review could also spill beyond paid hours. They keep that harmful possibility beside the benefit.

Six of ten additional changed jobs have unresolved decisions. The team prepares a shared list and paid meeting time. Maya will attend; deputy cover is still unfinished. The weakness stays visible.

Before the trial starts on 1 February, they record an expectation: at least thirty-two of forty anticipated requests receive prompt decisions, and no more than twelve of eighty orders go out late. This first-trial target does not replace the stricter completion criterion. The team deliberately shares its first reviewed release.

After one week, six of forty orders are late. Seventeen of twenty decisions are reported prompt. Maya suggests expanding, but they continue the same trial and check the definition.

Some acknowledgments had counted as authorized answers. Checking timestamps changes the first week's decision count from seventeen to eleven out of twenty. The earlier report remains beside its correction. Anyone returning can see why the apparent success changed.

The complete fortnight produces twenty-one prompt decisions out of forty, below thirty-two expected. Sixteen of eighty orders are late, above twelve expected. Eight of ten meetings took place; Maya missed two. Paper supply also worsened.

Imani identifies two unpaid preparation hours. Paying and recording them raises the second week's overtime from seventeen to nineteen hours, above the ceiling. Only ten people answer positively about speaking up; sixteen of twenty respond. The four missing answers remain unknown. Expansion pauses.

An AI brief calls staff reluctant to use the process. Imani objects: people used it, but nobody present could authorize an answer. Stephen withdraws the interpretation. Holding a meeting did not guarantee a decision.

The revised arrangement lets Ben and Rosa settle routine replans within written limits when they arrive. Morning meetings handle exceptions. A named authorized deputy and paid preparation time must also be available. The team gives fresh agreement to this changed trial.

Before 22 February, Maya defines the limits and names the deputy. Maya and Rosa allocate paid preparation time, taking two hours from nonurgent quotation work. Imani checks the burden. Quote delays stay on the watch list. Completing these actions prepares a trial; it does not establish its effects.

The next fortnight contains days under both arrangements. Fourteen of eighty orders are late; twenty-four of forty decisions are prompt. The report stays, but it cannot fairly evaluate the revised approach on its own.

Before March begins, they record a new expectation for 1–12 March: at least thirty-two prompt decisions out of forty, no more than eight late orders out of eighty, and all original safeguards. They share the reviewed correction and revised trial as their second release.

The full revised window produces thirty-four prompt decisions out of forty. During Maya's two-day absence, five of six requests still receive prompt answers. Authority is available without waiting for her return.

Seventy-five of eighty jobs have complete instructions at their slot. No comparable readiness baseline was collected, so the size of any improvement is unknown. Restored paper supply and slightly easier jobs also remain possible explanations for the delivery result.

Eight of eighty orders are late. Overtime is sixteen and fifteen hours; sixteen people answer positively about speaking up, with nineteen responses. No unpaid work is reported. This is one qualifying window. The team continues unchanged.

The second window produces thirty-five prompt decisions and seven late orders. Overtime is fourteen and fifteen hours; seventeen people answer positively, with nineteen responses. No unpaid work is reported. Both nonoverlapping March windows meet the original criteria. Their cause is still open to examination.

Active support ends on 2 April. Rosa inherits the purpose, revisions, predictions and open questions behind the arrangement. Ben supplies order figures, Maya checks paid hours and Imani maintains the anonymous pulse and private route for concerns. The third reviewed release marks handover; the working record can still change.

Unpaid work or reprisal concerns require immediate review and an offer of independent facilitation. Two consecutive failed delivery or speaking-up windows invite Stephen back. The group can ask sooner.

In May, nine of eighty orders go out late. Prompt decisions still pass at thirty-three of forty, and overtime stays within limits. Speaking up slips to fifteen positive answers, with eighteen responses. Deputy cover was reassigned twice; a new customer sends more late changes. One employee says challenging dates feels like holding everyone up.

The team restores cover, offers a voluntary conversation and schedules another review for 28 May. This is one failed window; the agreed two-window return trigger has not been reached. March's qualifying results remain on the record beside May's warning.

The next review has not happened.
