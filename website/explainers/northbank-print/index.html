The process illustrated here

OrientAgree a workable issue and original criteriaAssessExamine environment and capacities, including unknownsAnalyzeCompare demands with capacities and choose a priorityAdjustUse the selected group method to choose feasible actionRepeatReassess, revise and check the agreed endpoint

The October summary is the procedural reference: Orient and Assess on pages 4–6; Analyze on page 6; Adjust and the ACT group matrix on pages 6–8; Repeat and termination on page 8. AP is a flexible meta-protocol. This group chooses ACT; other engagements may choose another suitable method. The typical ACT order shown is A, B, 1, 2, 3, 4 and C. Its sequence can vary with the group.

The capacity choice shows the summary’s typical guidance: a more volatile environment emphasises adaptability, a more hazardous one robustness, and one that is neither permits coordination. Following the summary’s suggestions for organisations wary of vulnerability, the matrix uses plain business wording and starts with a lighter pass. The group goes deeper when the trial shows the leader’s worry holding authority in place, and the leader goes first in naming difficult thoughts.

AP builds on earlier work. The eight coordination principles are ProSocial World’s core design principles, which draw on Elinor Ostrom’s study of how communities govern shared resources. The group ACT matrix comes from ProSocial World, where David Sloan Wilson, Paul Atkins and Steven Hayes combined Ostrom’s insights with Acceptance and Commitment Therapy. The Orient criteria come from Convergent Facilitation, as described by Roni Wiener, Magda Barańska and Miki Kashtan.

Northbank uses a facilitated in-person discussion with private reflection and voluntary contributions. The source notes that ACT and EFT require training and practice; these scenes do not establish facilitation competence. The source provides no universal requirement for Northbank’s numerical thresholds, two-window endpoint or later monitoring cadence.

AP and the supporting Reason Commons tools

The environment-capacity comparison and group choice organize the work. The causal and future-reality fragments help question explanations and expected consequences. Preserved predictions, corrected reports, wording history and action dependencies support review. These are supporting Reason Commons practices; AP does not require every group to use these trees or records.

Here the current-reality tree connects the waiting decision to a rule, and the rule to a worry Maya named in the matrix. That lets the delegation and the ACT work address the same cause. Repeat takes up half the story because that is where a shared record helps most: AP says to repeat until the agreed criteria are met, and the record keeps predictions, corrections and criteria in view across each pass.

The original measures

Late dispatches

Orders dispatched after the original promised date divided by all standard repeat orders due. A revised promise does not erase a miss.

Conclude after two consecutive non-overlapping fortnights at ≤10%, each with at least 60 orders, and all guardrails met. One failure prompts review; two consecutive failures invite renewed facilitation.

Prompt decisions

Requests with an authorized decision communicated to the customer by the end of the next working day divided by all changed-order requests received. An acknowledgment alone is not a decision.

At least 80%. Two weeks below threshold require investigation before expansion.

Paid working time

Total paid overtime across all 22 employees, reported separately for each week. Check for unrecorded work as well.

Pilot ceiling 18 hours in each week. Any unpaid work pauses expansion immediately; an excess prompts adjustment.

Speaking up

Anonymous yes/no: “I can raise an unrealistic delivery commitment without penalty.” Twenty employees are eligible; the two directors are excluded. Missing answers remain missing.

At least 16 yes answers and at least 18 responses. A lower result requires review; any reprisal concern receives immediate attention.
