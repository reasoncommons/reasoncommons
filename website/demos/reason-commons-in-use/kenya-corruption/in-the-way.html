How Kenya scored in 2014

Two framing observations from the brief: where Kenya stood on corruption indices, and a history of anti-corruption promises followed by scandal. No causal connections are drawn.

Draws on Samira Lindner, Transparency International (document).

Outline

  • ObservationAs of 2013, Kenya ranked 136th of 177 countries on Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index and had sat between the 10th and 20th percentile for control of corruption in the World Bank's governance indicators since 1996.From Samira Lindner, Transparency International
  • ObservationCorruption and looting of state assets were widespread under one-party rule after independence; a government elected in 2002 on an anti-corruption platform was itself caught up in major scandals within two years.From Samira Lindner, Transparency International

Part 2 of 4

Why senior officials went unpunished

Diluted laws, a commission that could not prosecute and overloaded courts together let senior officials escape conviction, and public trust fell. Candidate causes, as of 2014.

Part 4 of 4

Party money, procurement and the police

Patronage parties needed opaque money, which the brief links to procurement scandals and vote-buying. Weak police oversight left the police the most bribed institution.

Draws on Samira Lindner, Transparency International (document).

Outline

  • ProblemAs of 2013, petty bribery was pervasive: 70% of Global Corruption Barometer respondents said they had paid a bribe to at least one of eight public services in the past year.From Samira Lindner, Transparency International
    • ↑ contributes to
      • ProblemAs of 2013, the police were the institution most affected: 95% of respondents saw them as corrupt and 77% said they had paid them a bribe.From Samira Lindner, Transparency International
