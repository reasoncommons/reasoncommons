Overview of corruption and anti-corruption in Kenya · Anti-Corruption Helpdesk brief

A 16-page brief by Samira Lindner of Transparency International, dated 15 October 2014 and reviewed by Marie Chêne, Casey Kelso and Samuel Kimeu (Transparency International Kenya). It answers a European Commission query on corruption in Kenya with a focus on public financial management, updating a 2012 U4 Helpdesk answer. It draws on indices, surveys and reports from 2011 to 2014. A description, not a programme

The brief answers a European Commission question about the state of corruption, with a focus on public financial management (p. 1). It describes far more than it recommends. The goal and most of the proposed changes are therefore the AI’s inferences from gaps the brief identifies, such as a commission without power to prosecute (pp. 10–11) and asset declaration removed from the Leadership and Integrity Act (p. 9), and each says so. The two dilemmas are ones the brief records: repaying donors for stolen school funds (pp. 5–6) and settling out of court (p. 8). Inspect an inferred change →
