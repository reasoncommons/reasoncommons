Conflict 1 of 6

Institutional strength and democratic integrity

A cooperative needs enough capacity to survive, but concentrating authority to get it erodes the member control that made it worth building.

Draws on Leif Eriksson (posts 29, 32, 36; essays) and Robert Bunge (posts 28, 30).

Assumptions under the arrows: Institutional strength and democratic integrity

  1. 1

    The required coordination and expertise are obtainable only, or at substantially lower viable cost, through concentrated authority.

    Behind arrow 1 (is needed for) · assumed by AI assistant

  2. 2

    The same decisions at the same scale and time must be either centrally controlled or genuinely controlled by members; no workable division or accountable delegation is available.

    Behind arrow 2 (conflicts with) · assumed by AI assistant

  3. 3

    Members cannot retain substantive control if those decisions are transferred to managers or central bodies.

    Behind arrow 3 (is needed for) · assumed by AI assistant

Conflict 2 of 6

AI capability and resilient autonomy

A community may need AI to keep up with rivals who use it, yet its essential functions must keep working when AI, power or a provider fails.

Draws on Leif Eriksson (posts 20, 33; essay) and Robert Bunge (posts 28, 30, 34).

Assumptions under the arrows: AI capability and resilient autonomy

  1. 1

    AI provides a decisive advantage for this function, and a non-AI or optional-AI arrangement cannot meet the requirement.

    Behind arrow 1 (is needed for) · assumed by AI assistant

  2. 2

    No tested fallback, bounded role or diversified architecture can reconcile these requirements for the same function at the same time.

    Behind arrow 2 (conflicts with) · assumed by AI assistant

  3. 3

    AI access cannot be made sufficiently dependable, locally controlled and replaceable for the selected essential function.

Conflict 3 of 6

Unilateral protection and common security

Deterrence seems to demand more of a contested capability, while shared survival demands reciprocal limits on that same capability.

Draws on Leif Eriksson (posts 23, 26) and Robert Bunge (posts 21, 24, 25).

Assumptions under the arrows: Unilateral protection and common security

  1. 1

    Superior or matching relative destructive capacity is the only credible protection.

    Behind arrow 1 (is needed for) · assumed by AI assistant

  2. 2

    Reciprocal restraints cannot be verified, enforced or maintained against powerful defectors while protecting weaker parties.

    Behind arrow 2 (conflicts with) · assumed by AI assistant

  3. 3

    Unchecked competitive accumulation increases mutual insecurity.

    Behind arrow 3 (is needed for) · assumed by AI assistant

Conflict 5 of 6

Controlled engagement and trustworthy relationships

A service needs funding to last, but engineering people’s attachment to secure it undermines the free choice that trust depends on.

Draws on Leif Eriksson (post 13; essays) and Robert Bunge (post 19).

Assumptions under the arrows: Controlled engagement and trustworthy relationships

  1. 1

    Financial or institutional continuity requires manipulating engagement, attachment or compliance.

    Behind arrow 1 (is needed for) · assumed by AI assistant

  2. 2

    No funding, ownership or accountability arrangement can sustain the service through benefits people can freely recognise and choose.

    Behind arrow 2 (conflicts with) · assumed by AI assistant

  3. 3

    Justified and generative trust require meaningful agency and correction, rather than manufactured confidence alone.

    Behind arrow 3 (is needed for) · assumed by AI assistant

Conflict 6 of 6

Success between institutions and integrity within

Surviving and gaining influence means building what the existing order rewards, but letting its pressures shape the institution erodes the values and relationships it exists for. Leif’s countercurrent’s paradox of success; it may lie behind four of the other five.

Draws on Leif Eriksson (posts 20, 26, 29, 38; essays).

Assumptions under the arrows: Success between institutions and integrity within

  1. 1

    Only the existing order's selection pressures decide which institutions survive and become influential.

    Behind arrow 1 (is needed for) · assumed by AI assistant

  2. 2

    The selection pressures are fixed: what wins between institutions does not reward what sustains emancipatory practice within them.

    Behind arrow 2 (conflicts with) · assumed by AI assistant

  3. 3

    Capabilities adopted in order to compete reshape internal relationships, priorities and sense of what is possible.

    Behind arrow 3 (is needed for) · assumed by AI assistant
