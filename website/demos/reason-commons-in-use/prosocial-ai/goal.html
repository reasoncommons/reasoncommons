Part 1 of 2

Flourishing at every scale

The authors judge AI by its effects on people, organisations, societies and the planet. Each scale has its own conditions, drawn from Section 4.

Part 2 of 2

Make cooperation pay

The authors' central claim: prosocial AI needs institutions that reward cooperation, not heroic sacrifice. Ostrom's core design principles, applied to AI, are the proposed conditions; worldviews come with them.

Draws on Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson (document).

Outline

  • Success factorInstitutional arrangements make cooperation more rewarding than extraction, so prosocial AI does not depend on self-sacrifice against competitive pressure.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
    • ↑ is needed for
      • ConditionAffected communities, not only shareholders and executives, agree what an AI system is for and whose interests it serves.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
    • ↑ is needed for
      • ConditionThose who contribute data, labour, creativity and resources receive proportional benefits, and costs are not pushed onto vulnerable people.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
    • ↑ is needed for
      • ConditionThose affected by AI rules take part in making them, including the choice not to build or deploy a system at all.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
    • ↑ is needed for
      • ConditionAlgorithmic systems are publicly audited and their effects on capability are reported, with escalating consequences for violations.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionAI's effects on human capability can be measured, so enhancement and extraction can be told apart.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
    • ↑ is needed for
      • ConditionAffected people have accessible channels to challenge harmful deployments, with transparent adjudication and real remedies.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
    • ↑ is needed for
      • ConditionStakeholder bodies hold real decision-making power over AI deployment, not consultation rights alone.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
    • ↑ is needed for
      • ConditionGovernance is nested across scales: groups coordinate purpose, equity and monitoring between them, while lower levels keep authority within their own domains.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
  • Success factorThose who design, govern and use AI hold worldviews that value capability, interdependence and effects across scales, and treat AI's direction as a collective choice rather than an inevitability.From Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson
