Evolving Prosocial AI · How We Shape the Systems that Shape AI - and Us

A 67-page manuscript by Paul Atkins and David Sloan Wilson of ProSocial World, version of 28 June 2026, accepted for Cambridge Elements in Applied Evolutionary Science. It argues that worldviews, acting as cultural replicators, set the selection pressures that decide whether AI extracts from or strengthens cooperation at individual, organisational, societal and planetary scales, and proposes Ostrom’s core design principles as the institutional answer. An earlier draft of 6 March 2026 was circulated on Substack; this later version supersedes it. Page numbers refer to the PDF.

An evolutionary argument about AI

The book treats worldviews as cultural replicators that persist by reshaping what gets rewarded, and AI systems as agents whose behaviour carries those worldviews back into the people around them (pp. 8–9, 11–12). Four extractive worldviews produce what the authors call a cancer pattern, success at one level at the expense of the levels above; four prosocial worldviews, put into practice through Ostrom’s core design principles, could produce the life pattern instead (pp. 15–20, 27, 35–37). The authors’ own cautions

The book warns that evolutionary ideas have been used to justify colonial harm, and that principles derived from studying communities should not override those communities’ own knowledge (p. 41). It reports mixed evidence that helpful AI can weaken a team’s shared understanding (p. 30), and that bridging fact-checks arrive too late to stop early spread (p. 33). Evidence the book cites

Findings the authors draw from other work, such as Ostrom’s Nepal irrigation studies and vTaiwan’s legislative record, appear as attributed observations. They have not been checked here, and some cited studies are preprints or under review (pp. 21–22). The authors say their examples show proof of concept at particular scales, skew toward Western and democratic settings, and that integration across all levels awaits rigorous testing (pp. 37, 48). What counts as progress?

No group, owner, baseline or observed outcome is established. The action sequence follows the book’s closing suggestions for researchers, technologists, policymakers and funders (p. 49); its existing conditions are illustrative, and nobody is assigned.
