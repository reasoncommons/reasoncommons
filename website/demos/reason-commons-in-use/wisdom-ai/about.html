Wisdom and AI

A Second Renaissance research agenda led by Rufus Pollock of Life Itself. One governing question, four core questions and their subquestions explore alignment, risk and pace, broader impacts and consciousness. Wisdom traditions means especially Buddhism in this source, while inviting others. It asks questions rather than establishing answers. Captured 25 October 2026.

The source’s research questions

How should we approach the development of AI wisely, and what do wisdom traditions offer?

93 questions in four strands, preserving the source hierarchy. Branches group questions; they do not assert causal relationships. Captured 2026-10-25. The repeated summary is not duplicated.

    • Source questionWhat would it mean for AI to be aligned, or good?
      • Source questionAligned to what: instructions, preferences, wellbeing, values, the good?

      • Source questionAligned to whom, and who decides?
      • Source questionMust AI be wise, or is "safe and corrigible" enough?
    • Source questionHow is it attempted today, and does it work?
      • Source questionWhat are the main approaches (rules and constitutions, preference learning, character training), and which ethical traditions do they implicitly embody?
      • Source questionWhat does the evidence show, both successes and failures?
      • Source questionCan we verify what a model has actually learnt?
        • Source questionCan we read a model's reasoning, and is it faithful?
        • Source questionCan models report their own states reliably?
        • Source questionWhat can interpretability show?
      • Source questionWhat is the strongest case that current methods will scale, and how convincing is it?
    • Source questionIs the target (goodness or wisdom) the kind of thing that can be specified or trained?
      • Source questionWhat do we know about how humans become good or wise?
      • Source questionHow does training an AI compare with how humans become good, and what, if anything, is the AI analogue?
    • Source questionWhat do wisdom traditions offer?
      • Source questionWhy these traditions, which strands, and how do we decide when they conflict with each other or with secular ethics?
      • Source questionWhich teachings bear on alignment, and what would each change in practice?
      • Source questionWhere do the analogies break down, and what can traditions not tell us?
    • Source questionSo: can we align AI, and what is the realistic role of wisdom traditions?
Risk and pace: given existential risk and the chance alignment fails, how should we approach AI development, and what do wisdom traditions suggest?
  • Source questionRisk and pace: given existential risk and the chance alignment fails, how should we approach AI development, and what do wisdom traditions suggest?
    • Source questionWhat are the catastrophic and existential risks?
      • Source questionWhat kinds: loss of control, misuse, irreversible power concentration, gradual disempowerment?
      • Source questionWhat evidence is there that these are already emerging?
      • Source questionHow likely and how soon, and how much do experts disagree?
    • Source questionWhy is the world racing anyway?
      • Source questionWhat drives the race: competition between labs and states, money, prestige, "if we don't, others will"?
      • Source questionWhat evidence is there of race dynamics, and of restraint?
      • Source questionWhy is the race so hard to escape? Are there deeper cultural or psychological drivers?
    • Source questionShould we change pace or trajectory, and can we?
      • Source questionIs a slower or different trajectory desirable (pause, pacing, steering, differential development), weighed against the case for speed (ceding ground, forgone benefits)?
      • Source questionIs it feasible? What mechanisms exist (auditing, compute governance, treaties), and what are the precedents of collective restraint?
      • Source questionWho decides?
    • Source questionWhat do wisdom traditions offer here?
      • Source questionWhat do they say we should do? Is the normative answer clear?
      • Source questionDo they help with how, i.e. solving the collective action problem, and if so through what (e.g. a shift in worldview), or is this where they offer less?
      • Source questionHow would a wise process make this choice, beyond weighing a trade-off?
    • Source questionSo: what should each actor (labs, states, communities, individuals) do, and at what pace?
