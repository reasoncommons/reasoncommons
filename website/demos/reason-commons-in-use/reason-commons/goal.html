Question 1 of 4 · The goal

What are we trying to achieve?

The goal, and what has to be true for it.

Statements
26
Connections

How to read it. Read from the top down: the goal needs each condition beneath it.

GoalSuccess factorCondition

Outline

  • GoalA reason commons is emerging and widely used: people, with AI, build shared tree-structured reasoning (such as Logical Thinking Process maps) and weave it into discussion and work, so discourse is more constructive and people find the leverage points and achieve their goals together far more effectively.
    • ↑ is needed for
      • Success factorAI-supported tools make creating and revising reasoning trees easy, whether people build them explicitly or the trees are drawn semi-automatically from their existing discussion.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionA person can get a usable first draft of a tree in minutes, whether starting from scratch or from material they already have.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionPeople who don't know the method can build a tree that holds up when others review it.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionChanging a tree is quick.
    • ↑ is needed for
      • Success factorReasoning trees improve discussion where it already happens (chats, documents, meetings, social platforms), surfacing the relevant structure alongside the conversation.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionA tree can be shown and updated inside the places people already talk, without moving the conversation elsewhere.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionPeople in a discussion can point to a specific point in the tree and respond to it.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionKeeping the tree current adds little effort for anyone in the discussion.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionSomeone joining a discussion can grasp a tree's main point, and where their own point fits, within a minute.
    • ↑ is needed for
      • Success factorReal people and groups use it regularly and see better decisions and results, starting with Rufus, David and Second Renaissance.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionRufus and David run their own real decisions and plans through trees as a habit.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionSecond Renaissance uses trees on at least one real, recurring decision or plan.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionAt least one named group beyond Rufus, David and Second Renaissance uses it without either of them in the room.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionUsers can point to a specific decision or result that came out better because of a tree.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionUsing a tree on a decision takes less effort than the value people get from it.
    • ↑ is needed for
      • Success factorIt is valuable to one person or a pair from day one, without needing a commons first, and gets more valuable for a team, an organization and the wider world.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionOne person gets something useful in their first session, without a group or existing trees.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionA tree one person starts can be shared with a pair or team and grow there without being rebuilt.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionEach added contributor makes a tree more useful to the others, not noisier.
    • ↑ is needed for
      • Success factorTrees are published, connected and evolved collectively, within organizations and in public, so people build on each other's reasoning, share core reference trees, and find others working toward the same goals.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionTrees can be read, copied and reused outside the tool that made them.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionAnyone can propose a change to a tree; its owners can accept or decline it, and every change records who made it and why.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionA tree can link to and reuse parts of other trees.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionPeople can find trees, and the people behind them, working toward the same or related goals.
        • ↑ is needed for
          • ConditionOwners control whether a tree is private to a group or public.
