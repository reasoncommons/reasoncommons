A framework and a protocol

The summary places Adaptive Persistence, a protocol for cooperation, in a larger Persistence Framework with a meta-ethics, Procedural Ethics, and a meta-cosmology, Persistent Systems Theory. It says the ethics and the theory are not essential to fostering cooperation but help sustain it in the long run (summary, p. 2). This map follows the protocol: three questions about a group’s capacities, an assessment of its environment, and five steps to reduce the mismatch between them.

The case studies

The summary reports four cases from the author’s own facilitation, using lighter ProSocial variations of the method: a genomics nonprofit, a small company and its contractor, a jazz quartet and two leaders of a foodways nonprofit (pp. 8–9). They are the author’s reports, not independent evidence, and the summary itself asks for further cases in competitive, innovative or hazardous settings. Inspect the attributed observation →

What counts as progress?

No group, facilitator, owner, baseline or observed outcome is established. The protocol asks a group to agree success criteria when it orients and to stop once they are met; those criteria belong to the group that tries it. Conditions in the action sequence are illustrative, not observations of an actual group.
