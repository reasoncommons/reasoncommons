Entry 01 / 1311 January 2027Orient

A request to make production more responsive becomes a shared goal with explicit limits.

Maya opens the intake meeting with “We need production to become more responsive.” Rosa hears a familiar demand to work faster. Ben says customers increasingly change specifications at short notice. Imani asks whether the engagement will simply make those changes easier to pass on to operators.

Entry 02 / 1318 January 2027Assess

What is happening here?

Interviews and actual orders produce competing explanations; one stays visibly disputed.

Stephen combines six short interviews, an optional questionnaire and existing order records. Twenty of 22 people provide some input. Absence is not endorsement. The baseline shows 20/80 dispatches late, 12/40 prompt decisions, average overtime of 18 hours, and 12 positive voice answers from 20 responses.

Customers’ purchasing systems may make short lead times increasingly normal over two to five years. Supplier dependence creates disruption exposure, but no immediate survival crisis is identified. Machinery safeguards remain mandatory. The office hears customers’ deadlines while the workshop faces materials and transport constraints: heterogeneity matters.

Stephen’s facilitation

Stephen asks the group to distinguish observations from explanations. Coordination concerns center on decision-making and fair costs; adaptability on combining scattered information and learning; robustness on dependence on Maya. The AI drafts an explanation blaming delayed information from sales. Rosa recognizes examples, but Ben supplies timestamped emails that challenge the generalization.
